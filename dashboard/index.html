Module 1

EAT-26 risk pattern classifier

EAT-26 is a validated screening tool, not a diagnostic one. Real respondent-level data isn't public, so this dataset is synthetic — generated to follow published EAT-26 subscale patterns (Garner et al., 1982) rather than copied from real patients. This is a portfolio ML pipeline, not a clinical tool.

Confusion is concentrated between Bulimic and Binge patterns — both share elevated Bulimia/Food-Preoccupation scores; Dieting severity is the main separator the model relies on. Oral Control score is the single most important feature overall, consistent with literature on impulse control as a key differentiator for binge-type presentations.

Module 2 — Real NHS Digital data

Episodes by subtype, 2024-25
"Eating Disorder, Unspecified" is the second-largest category (28.8%) — a real coding-practice finding, not just a clinical prevalence one: a large share of episodes aren't coded to a specific subtype.

The clearest real trend in this data: Anorexia Nervosa episodes have fallen 25% since 2021-22, while Binge-type and Bulimic-spectrum categories have grown substantially. Worth investigating further with clinical context.

Module 3

Trend & forecast

Total (F50, all subtypes) admissions, England, 2021-22 through 2024-25, with a 3-year forward projection.

4 annual points is a thin sample for forecasting. The held-out check (predicting 2024-25 from the 3 prior years) had a ~1,200-episode error — treat the forecast as directional, not precise.
Module 4 — ONS mortality

Deaths by region, 2024

ICD-10 F50-coded deaths, England & Wales, by region, 2024. Source: ONS via Nomis.

These are very small counts (max 9 per region). Normal year-to-year noise can easily produce differences this size — don't over-read the regional ranking as a "riskier region" finding.
